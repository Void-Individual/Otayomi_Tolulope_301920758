# Portfolio 1 — Relational design and enforcement

**Tolulope Otayomi · 301920758 · Palm Court Guesthouse**

The model has four business tables: `guest`, `room_category`, `room` and `reservation`. `demo_clock` is a separate technical fixture table. The [ER diagram](Otayomi_Tolulope_301920758_P1_ERDiagram.svg) and [schema](Otayomi_Tolulope_301920758_P1_Schema.sql) describe the same tables and relationships.

## Relations, keys and participation

| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `guest`         | PK `guest_code text`; `display_name text`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Both required. One guest may have zero or many reservations; each reservation requires one guest. Names are not assumed unique.                                                                              |

| `room`          | PK `room_number text`; FK `category_code text`; `capacity integer`                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | All required. One room may have zero or many historical reservations; every reservation requires one room. Dates determine whether those reservations conflict.                                              |
| `reservation`   | PK `reservation_ref text`; FKs `guest_code text`, `room_number text`; `arrival date`, `departure date`, `party_size integer`, `status text`, `agreed_nightly_rate ngn_amount`, `booked_on date`, `status_changed_on date`, `created_at timestamptz`, `status_changed_at timestamptz` | All required. No nullable relationship or draft state. No additional candidate key is assumed: one guest may book several rooms and a cancelled booking can share its room/dates with its replacement.       |
| `demo_clock`    | PK `singleton boolean` constrained to true; `as_of_date date`                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Required fields. At most one row; schema installation inserts it. No foreign-key relationship to business entities. The operator reads it but cannot alter it. Missing clock data causes operations to fail. |

Codes and numbers are natural demonstration identifiers, not generated integer IDs. Stable identifiers make the fixtures and evidence easy to cross-reference. Foreign keys use PostgreSQL's default `NO ACTION`; history does not cascade away when a parent is deleted.

## Representing dates and money

The stay is `[arrival, departure)`. The number of nights is `departure - arrival`; a per-night expansion can derive the dates from that interval. There is no physical room-night allocation table. One constrained reservation row therefore reserves the entire stay atomically and cannot contain a partial set of nights.

The guesthouse uses one quoted nightly price throughout a stay. `agreed_nightly_rate` is captured once in the reservation and applies to every night. Storing that identical rate on separate night rows would duplicate a value determined by the reservation reference. Booking value is computed, not stored. The original value of a cancelled booking remains available, but it must be excluded from non-cancelled booking totals.

`ngn_amount` uses exact `numeric` with checks for a positive value, the documented upper bound and no fractional precision beyond two places. An unconstrained numeric domain is intentional: `numeric(12,2)` would round a value such as `20.001` before a check could reject its excessive precision. Nullability is enforced separately on each required column. NaN and infinity fail the bounded amount rule.

`booked_on` and `status_changed_on` are scenario dates. `created_at` and `status_changed_at` record the actual database clock and are set by the trigger, not supplied as fictional historical timestamps. Fixture replay can therefore produce different audit timestamps without changing the business results.

## Mapping rules to enforcement

| Rule                                               | Mechanism                                                                                                         | Evidence and portfolio links                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identifiers, required fields and references        | PK, alternate UNIQUE key, NOT NULL, format CHECKs and FKs                                                         | Duplicate guest/category/reference, missing name/party, unknown category/guest/room.                                                                                                                                        |
| Finite, positive-length stay; no past new bookings | Date CHECK plus `reservation_guard`, using the controlled business date                                           | Equal/reversed dates, infinity and past arrival rejected.                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Positive party within fixed room capacity          | Positive CHECK; trigger reads capacity; room update/delete trigger freezes inventory                              | Zero party, over-capacity, exact capacity and forbidden capacity edit.                                                                                                                                                      |
| No overlapping non-cancelled stays                 | Partial GiST exclusion constraint on room equality and range overlap; `btree_gist` supplies text equality support | Equal, contained and partial overlaps rejected; adjacent stays and different rooms accepted; completed history still blocks overlaps.                                                                                       |
| Full booking or no booking                         | One INSERT stores interval, state and captured rate; PostgreSQL statement/transaction atomicity                   | A failed multi-step operation leaves neither its new guest nor reservation. The [P3 transcripts](../portfolio-3-transactions-concurrency/Otayomi_Tolulope_301920758_P3_README.md) add observed concurrent-session evidence. |
| Valid lifecycle and retained history               | Guard checks initial/next state, business date and immutable terms; deletes rejected                              | Early completion, late cancellation, terminal-state reversal, edits and deletion rejected; valid cancellation/completion accepted.                                                                                          |
| Cancellation frees nights                          | Exclusion predicate omits only `CANCELLED`; original row retained                                                 | A replacement succeeds after cancellation and the old quoted price remains.                                                                                                                                                 |
| Preserve agreed prices                             | Trigger snapshots current rate on INSERT; immutable reservation terms thereafter                                  | Changing a category rate affects the next booking but not an earlier one.                                                                                                                                                   |
| Controlled operations                              | `tolu_guesthouse_operator` has SELECT and EXECUTE on booking/status functions, without table DML or TRUNCATE      | Operator cannot change clock, insert directly or truncate history; can book using the API.                                                                                                                                  |
| Period/value reporting semantics                   | Derived nights and values; reporting query must clip interval and filter status                                   | [P2 queries and assertions](../portfolio-2-query-optimisation/Otayomi_Tolulope_301920758_P2_README.md) verify clipped periods and zero-count groups.                                                                        |
| Same-case NoSQL events                             | P4 event snapshots, no automatic synchronisation                                                                  | [P4 validation and reconciliation](../portfolio-4-nosql/Otayomi_Tolulope_301920758_P4_README.md) verify the separate document model.                                                                                        |

The exclusion constraint is database-enforced. The capacity and state rules need triggers because they involve other rows or earlier values. PostgreSQL documents that ordinary CHECK constraints cannot reliably enforce cross-row/table conditions. See [constraints](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/ddl-constraints.html), [range constraints](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/rangetypes.html#RANGETYPES-CONSTRAINT) and [btree_gist](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/btree-gist.html).

## Operations and limits

`guesthouse.reserve_room(reference, guest, room, arrival, departure, party)` creates a confirmed booking. `guesthouse.change_status(reference, status)` performs an allowed transition. Both are `SECURITY DEFINER`, use a fixed search path with `pg_catalog` first and `pg_temp` last, and reference qualified tables. Default PUBLIC execution is revoked; only the designated operator is granted the two operations and the business-date reader. These choices follow PostgreSQL's [function security guidance](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/sql-createfunction.html#SQL-CREATEFUNCTION-SECURITY).

The administrator loads fixtures and changes category rates and scenario dates. That privileged owner can also change schema definitions or disable protections; the portfolio does not claim protection against its database administrator. The operator is a `NOLOGIN` role tested with `SET ROLE`, not a deployed authentication service. The fixture clock must be replaced with a protected real business-time policy in a production adaptation.

The current tests are sequential. They verify the exclusion rule and failed-operation cleanup but do not establish a measured two-session concurrency outcome or production performance. Those are separate P3 and P2 exercises.
